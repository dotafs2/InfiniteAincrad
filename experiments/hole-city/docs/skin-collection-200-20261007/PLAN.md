## Direction

The user rejects plain recolors, requires visible rival-swallow effects, and asks
for a commercially sensible 200-skin allocation followed by review of each color
tier. Interpret white/green/blue/purple/gold/prismatic as rarity badges, not body
palettes. A Common orange slice remains orange; an expensive skin need not cycle
through rainbow colors. Confirm actual palettes through individual samples.

This is a proposed starting allocation, not a demonstrated revenue optimum.
Preserve existing IDs and ownership; improve existing skins in place. The 200
includes existing skins, not 200 additions.

Common remains a small part of the collection; blue and purple supply the main
variety, while gold and Prismatic provide recognizable long-term goals. Low-tier
quality must make early ownership satisfying. Higher-tier distinction should
come from a stronger idea and cohesive motion, not merely more particles.
Tempest and Rainfall remain two of the twelve Prismatic slots.

Common now uses the `sculpted_common` quality contract. The remaining
`simple_trim` Uncommon contract still needs improvement in its own batch. Reusing construction
tools and effect batches is appropriate; changing only hue does not count as a
new authored skin. All skins preserve the same gameplay capabilities.

## Rival-swallow quality contract

There already is a generic rival burst for ordinary skins. Tempest and Rainfall
have dedicated handlers. Existing generic bursts often differ primarily by
palette; implementation existence does not establish visible quality.

Every tier must have a short, readable theme action plus themed fragments or a
surface response. Common can be simple: a biscuit bounce and chunky crumbs,
or a citrus squeeze and a juice fan. Item intake remains smaller and quicker;
rival intake is unmistakable at normal gameplay scale. The response must not
delay consumption, change scores, replay a swallowed model or hold the hole still.

Provisional active-particle design envelopes are 6–8 / 8–12 / 12–16 / 16–24 /
24–32 / 32–48 across the tiers. These are starting caps, not minimums or measured
performance promises. Use fixed pools, batched meshes and a small shared set of
motion families with authored theme geometry and timing. Measure complete-scene
cost before accepting any budget; do not allocate full effects on every event.

Acceptance for each representative skin:

1. Inspect the actual normal camera at level 1 and level 3, on light and dark
   ground, in a complete running map. Do not accept only a close-up display scene.
2. Show idle, item intake and rival intake in a muted gameplay clip. Rival feedback
   must remain visibly distinct for roughly 0.35–0.65 seconds without relying on
   sound, an enlarged camera or its rarity label.
3. Repeat with legitimate boost active. Use low/lateral motion rather than another
   concentric ring; avoid the name/level label covering the signature action.
   Existing weather gameplay exposes both label overlap and boost-ring competition.
4. Confirm unchanged collection timing, single reward and single visual descent.
   Preserve the accepted Rainfall handoff and all actual user saves.
5. Measure representative aggregate rendering cost, retire pooled effects cleanly,
   and seek native performance evidence before making a phone frame-rate claim.

## Collection economy

Keep the currently accepted economy during art approval:

- One draw costs 100 coins and grants an unowned skin while any remain.
- Coins equal floor(points / 10), so 1,000 points buys one draw. It is not a fixed
  one-draw reward for every round. The user's 971-point recording yields 97 coins.
- The verified entry offer is USD 1.99 for 100 diamonds; exchanging all of those
  diamonds gives 2,000 coins, or 20 draws. Spending on boosts changes that result.
- Starting with one skin, a full 200-skin collection needs 199 additional draws,
  costing 19,900 coins. At 97 base coins per round and 50 initial coins, that is
  205 similar rounds. Advertisements, multipliers and existing balances reduce it.
- Ten entry packs cost USD 19.90 and fund 200 draws. This is not the minimum
  purchase price for completion: other packs can offer better rates. It is also
  not developer net revenue. Do not promise 200 rounds or that price as fixed.

Rarity changes acquisition order, not the number of draws needed with no
duplicates. Do not reduce probabilities or introduce duplicates to compensate
for production cost without a separate product decision. Future content and
measured player interest should inform ongoing monetization.

Collection shares are not draw odds. Current base tier weights are
45 / 28 / 16 / 8 / 2.5 / 0.5 percent. The real offer removes empty tiers and
applies the existing 5 / 10 / 20 Epic / Legendary / Prismatic guarantees;
higher rewards also reset qualifying lower counters. Keep these unchanged for
this planning step. Before a later rollout, inspect early collection satisfaction,
skin equip frequency, draw conversion and time to a desired skin, then assess
whether a disclosed adjustment is justified. No player metrics were collected
or revenue forecast made in this task.

Keep the purchase-facing rates synchronized with actual eligible pools and
guarantees. Apple's [App Review Guidelines, section 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
require odds disclosure before purchasing randomized virtual items.
