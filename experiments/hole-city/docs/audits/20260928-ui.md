# UI and skin economy audit — v0.7.0

Audit date: 2026-09-28. Scope: current home, skin collection, HUD, results,
skin acquisition and player-facing progression. This is a review, not a
runtime change. No player saves were opened or changed.

## Prioritized findings

### UI-01 — P1: The home level badge never progresses

**Classification:** verified player-facing progression defect; the precise
reference level curve remains unverified.

`fidelity/game.gd:52` initializes `stage` to 1. Its only other runtime uses are
loading at line 416 and saving at line 451. `fidelity/ui.gd:470` presents this
value as `LVL`. Successful settlement at `fidelity/game.gd:533` grants coins but
does not advance it. The in-round `level_up()` at line 529 changes only a notice.

**Reproduction:** the isolated probe set stage 4, finished a 420-point round,
returned home and started again. Stage stayed 4 after both transitions. A new
player therefore remains at home LVL 1 regardless of victories. The badge
suggests lasting progress that does not exist; replay also resets the same
210-second round. This weakens the collection/round progression loop even
though coins work.

**Acceptance:** define the meaning and advancement rule for the home badge;
either implement that progression or remove the misleading level label. A
successful round must update the intended progress once, survive reload, and
be visible before replay. Failed/abandoned rounds must follow a documented rule.
Do not claim a matched Hole.io progression curve without observing it.

### UI-02 — P1: Special purchases hide gameplay-changing abilities

**Classification:** verified usability regression in the current collection.

`fidelity/game.gd:22` through line 26 define five different Special abilities:
pull, automatic catch, building movement, speed and sweep, with different
cooldowns. The collection at `fidelity/ui.gd:571` through line 586 draws only a
name and ownership state; `skill_desc` and `play_desc` are never displayed by
the current UI. Selecting a locked card at line 238 only says to use random
unlock. The first explanation during play is a short label such as `THRUM`,
`DRAW` or `AUTO` at line 650.

**Impact:** a player spends the same currency on items that alter play without
being told what they do. Automatic equipment after purchase can change the
controls and strategy unexpectedly. The earlier detail panel was removed to
match the grid, but its necessary gameplay information was not relocated.

**Acceptance:** each Special card or a lightweight details sheet must explain
its effect, automatic/manual trigger and cooldown before purchase and after
unlock. Cosmetic forms must be distinguishable. A first-use prompt must explain
the applicable in-game control without adding constant HUD clutter.

### UI-03 — P2: Reopening the library hides the equipped Special skin

**Classification:** verified navigation/state defect.

`fidelity/ui.gd:251` always sets `holes_tab = "classic"`; when the equipped skin
belongs to Special it also sets the preview to the first Classic form.

**Reproduction:** owned and equipped Shard, returned home, then reopened Holes.
The probe returned `opened_tab: "classic"`, `equipped_id: "shard"`,
`equipped_visible: false`. The visible grid has no SELECTED card, so users can
reasonably think their selection was lost even though gameplay still equips it.

**Acceptance:** reopen on the equipped skin's category, or persist the last
viewed category while showing an unambiguous equipped-skin summary. Verify this
for every owned form, after round return and after a cold save restore.

### UI-04 — P2: The skin selector cannot demonstrate animated skins

**Classification:** verified presentation gap; not a claim about the reference
game's exact animation behavior.

At `fidelity/ui.gd:289` the large selected preview is always hidden and disabled.
Every card at line 707 uses `SubViewport.UPDATE_ONCE`, then builds and stops its
model after `_process(.65)`. Even time-driven shaders receive only one rendered
frame. The unlock feedback at line 383 scales a frozen thumbnail rather than
showing its motion.

**Impact:** the requested differences between water, flame, accretion, plasma
and other effects cannot be assessed in the selection page. A static slice
does not communicate a skin whose main distinction is movement. This also
limits the current UI evidence: it cannot demonstrate the effect quality.

**Acceptance:** animate only the highlighted/selected card or a focused preview;
keep unselected cards one-shot and stop the active preview on exit. Show at
least one full effect cycle and verify each skin is visually distinct without
introducing multiple continuously rendered card viewports.

### UI-05 — P2: The reference collection hub is still incomplete

**Classification:** verified design/feature gap against directly observed
reference screens, not a broken transaction.

`fidelity/REFERENCE.md:24` records a Store with coin/gem balances and offers;
line 25 records Classic 1/27 and Special 0/17. Current navigation at
`fidelity/ui.gd:293` through line 310 provides Home/Holes/Play with no Store,
and `fidelity/game.gd:20` defines only ten total skins (five per category).
The home page has a single Holes button rather than the same persistent dock.
Gems are loaded and saved at `fidelity/game.gd:415`/450 but have no visible
wallet or acquisition/use loop on the current Web screens.

**Impact:** the tabs and green button now resemble the reference, but the
surrounding collection experience and content depth do not. Counts correctly
show 5/5; fabricating 27/17 would be worse. The empty sixth card position is a
consequence of the small implemented catalog, not a rendering bug.

**Acceptance:** establish one consistent home/collection/store navigation model
and implement only real, working virtual-currency offers if that store is in
scope. Show balances where they can be used. Expand the catalog with distinct
authored silhouettes and use truthful counts/scrolling. Reference paid offers
are study evidence, not authorization to integrate real purchases or ads.

### UI-06 — P2: Coin earning is opaque, and matching the target economy is unverified

**Classification:** verified current UI information gap plus an explicit
reference-verification gap; current transaction correctness is not disputed.

Current tuning is `COIN_PRICES = [50,70,90,110,140]` at
`fidelity/game.gd:68`. Settlement at line 539 is floor(score/10), plus 20 for a
nonempty completed round. The result at `fidelity/ui.gd:538` shows only the
total. When short of funds, line 598 says only to play rounds.

For example, the inspected loss fixture displays 27 points and +2 coins. A
420-point completion produces 62 coins; after using the initial 50 coins, that
one win is still short of the second Classic price of 70. These are exact
consequences of current tuning, not measurements of typical player results or
claims that the reference pays more. The project's own v0.7.0 report confirms
later prices/reward rules are authored rather than verified reference rules.

**Impact:** players cannot predict the effort needed for their next unlock or
understand why a short round paid zero/very few coins. Similar-looking buttons
do not establish the requested identical economy.

**Acceptance:** show score reward and completion bonus separately, explain the
earning rule in a compact help affordance, and show coins missing for the next
unlock. Record target-version prices and end-of-round rewards before claiming
economy parity. Balance first and second earned unlocks using actual playthrough
data from both maps instead of score fixtures alone.

## QA boundary and things that are working

The v0.7.0 transaction tests cover no duplicate draws, correct charges,
insufficient funds, save rollback, migration and restore. This audit found no
evidence that those mechanics are broken. Both categories and actual selection
are implemented; the problem in UI-03 is which category is shown on re-entry.

`tests/coin_skins_acceptance.gd:25`/28 and
`tests/ui_redesign_acceptance.gd:22`/25 construct mouse motion/button events.
They do not establish touchscreen behavior, device safe-area handling, browser
storage persistence or final GPU-rendered appearance. The parent QA report
owns those acceptance gaps. Passing thousands of assertions is useful evidence
of tested behavior, not proof of visual parity or a successful iPhone session.
