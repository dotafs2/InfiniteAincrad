# Sunny City reference audit — September 28, 2026

Scope: shipped v0.7.0 city, pedestrian motion, traffic and the first-round loop.
This is a read-only audit. No runtime, player save, deployment or foreground app
was changed. The reference is the directly observed ledger in
`fidelity/REFERENCE.md:10–25`; its later implementation paragraphs are historical.
The current source identity matches the v0.7.0 release manifest for all five
audited files recorded in `artifacts/reference-audit-20260928/city/source-identity.json`.

The browser remains unavailable. No rendered gameplay screenshot, browser play
session or phone FPS is claimed. Controller probes below exercise actual current
methods in a headless, isolated `--test` process. Their results are numerical
evidence of motion, not a visual judgment about animation quality.

## Findings, in recommended order

### 1. P1 — Normal walking never updates NPC heading at 60 Hz

**Verified bug.** `fidelity/catalog_city.gd:665` caps ordinary movement at
0.58 m/s, while line 668 rotates the body only when the frame displacement
squared exceeds 0.0001. At 60 Hz the maximum is `(0.58 / 60)^2 = 0.00009344`, so
ordinary walkers can never meet the condition. At 30 Hz they can.

The isolated ten-second probe found 56 ordinary citizens moving more than
0.1 m at both rates: **0 of 56 changed heading at 60 Hz, 56 of 56 at 30 Hz**.
One character moved 1.36 m while retaining its starting heading of 1.7 radians.
This supplies a concrete cause for sideways/backwards walking that can make the
rig appear broken despite correctly playing Walk clips. It does not prove that
the imported bone tracks themselves are malformed.

Existing movement QA runs the city controller with 0.1-second steps
(`tests/mobile_maps_acceptance.gd:55`), and its heading check at line 69 is for
cars, not people. Thus it misses this frame-rate dependency.

**Acceptance:** compare a velocity-based direction against body heading at
30/60/120 Hz. After the turn blend settles, every ordinary walking citizen must
face its actual horizontal velocity within 15 degrees. Keep the real 60 Hz
physics/collision acceptance separate from these controller checks.

### 2. P1 — Citizens can overlap completely during normal crowd movement

**Verified bug.** Living citizens have collision layer and mask zero
(`fidelity/catalog_city.gd:367–368`) and scripted movement directly changes their
positions (`:666–667`). There is no citizen separation in that path. Restricting
everyone to the sidewalk does not keep them apart.

The isolated 20-second, 60 Hz controller run found **57 distinct pairs** with
center spacing below their combined 0.46 m capsule diameter. The closest police
and thief pair reached **0.0 m** center separation at 12.9 seconds. This is an
overlap of the defined physical footprints; browser visual overlap has not been
captured. The sidewalk test at `tests/mobile_maps_acceptance.gd:58` covers region
membership, while lines 70–72 only check inter-vehicle overlap.

**Acceptance:** ordinary living pedestrians maintain at least 0.46 m pair
spacing (or an explicitly authored smaller visual radius) through wandering,
panic and chase. Add separation that preserves sidewalk boundaries and does not
prevent a person over a hole from switching to gravity. Check at 60 Hz for a
minute, including dense pockets and pursuit.

### 3. P2 — Sunny City still uses repeated rows rather than connected neighborhoods

**Verified layout fact; experiential impact is an inference.** The city has 48
buildings: 36 in six equal six-building rows, plus two side columns
(`fidelity/catalog_city.gd:427–432`). Its road plan is two fixed horizontal and
two fixed vertical axes (`fidelity/street_layout.gd:3–4`), and all 64 pedestrians
remain tied to four horizontal strips (`:6–10`, `catalog_city.gd:482–501`).
Street props are placed as catalog-variant rows (`catalog_city.gd:455–460`).

This explains why successful object-count and road-clearance checks do not
establish the user's desired city composition. The food map's newer connected
street atlas has not replaced this city layout. The exact target Hole.io map
topology was not measured, so this is a gap against the user's stated city
direction, not a claim that every reference map lacks grids.

**Acceptance:** author at least three recognizable connected districts with
different block sizes, one landmark/park per district, pedestrian routes that
continue around corners, and frontage related to street geometry. Review both
opening and grown-hole camera views; maintain the existing individual-drop and
road-clearance checks.

### 4. P2 — City traffic follows infinite straight lanes and visibly testable teleports

**Verified motion discontinuity; on-camera exposure not yet verified.** Each car
has a fixed axis and direction (`fidelity/street_layout.gd:17–24`). It never turns
at a junction. Crossing +/-47 teleports it to the opposite endpoint
(`fidelity/catalog_city.gd:601–608`), inside the game's +/-76 world bounds
(`fidelity/game.gd:19`).

A 60 Hz probe observed a **93.99 m jump in one frame**, from z=-46.99085 to z=47.
The existing lane and overlap tests permit this because the car remains on the
same lane. It is a discontinuity in the current controller, not evidence that
the browser currently displays the teleport from the opening camera.

**Acceptance:** use connected routes with continuous turning, or explicitly
off-camera spawn/despawn zones outside every supported camera frustum. Assert
frame displacement <= speed * delta plus tolerance while a vehicle is visible,
and keep car swallowing, intersection clearance and the absence of deadlocks.

### 5. P2 — The opening loop still differs from the directly observed target

**Verified reference gap, not a crash bug.** The recorded target starts at
04:00 with 500 PTS and a blue player (`fidelity/REFERENCE.md:18–19`). Current
code explicitly uses 210 seconds / 420 points (`fidelity/game.gd:15–18`) and
the default orange Core Shell tint (`:21`, applied at `:159`). The current skin
has an authored accretion effect rather than the observed plain blue opening.

These were intentional earlier design choices, but remain differences after
the request for close reference matching. Exact later growth thresholds,
reference opponent behavior and completion rewards have not been measured.

**Acceptance:** either implement the already observed 240-second / 500-point
opening with the matching default appearance, or obtain an explicit product
decision to keep the authored tuning. Do not claim unknown later thresholds or
economy details are exact. Measure time to first collection, first upgrade and
first car swallow during actual play before declaring equivalent pacing.

### 6. P1 validation gap — iPhone 14 sustained 60 FPS remains unverified

The current evidence is Mac headless CPU timing with rendering disabled
(`docs/performance-20260928.md:21–26`), not a failed device test. The city has
6.47–6.49 ms P95 in those CPU fixtures, but this omits WebAssembly, the GPU,
shadows, shader cost and thermal behavior. The release's 5,285 passing checks
therefore cannot establish the requested phone performance or visual parity.

**Acceptance:** capture a ten-minute iPhone 14 run on each map with original
quality settings, including growth, simultaneous destruction and skin effects.
Report real frame-time distribution, sustained FPS, visible stutter, thermal
trend and peak memory. Keep these results separate from native headless CPU
numbers. This audit does not assert that the current game fails 60 FPS.

## Existing fixes that should not be re-reported as current defects

Current residents do enter `begin_swallow` before another scripted fleeing step
(`fidelity/catalog_city.gd:631–638`), and their animation stops after physics
release (`:628–639`). Whole-city acceptance in the v0.7.0 release checks actual
collection receipts for individual shipped objects, including residents.
The old reference ledger's statement that residents cannot be swallowed is
obsolete. There is no evidence in this audit to revive that fixed allegation.

Likewise, road membership, vehicle heading and intersection overlap are covered
by current tests, and building fragments retain authored exterior geometry and
material. The defects above concern additional behavior these tests do not
cover, rather than invalidating all earlier fixes.
