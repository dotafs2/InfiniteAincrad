# Original map music

The score was rewritten on **2026-09-30** after the user found the original music loud and chaotic. These two original synthesized recordings use no downloaded music, samples, third-party recordings or cloud-generated audio. The entire melody, harmony, instrument design and mix are in [tools/build_music.py](../../tools/build_music.py).

Sunny City uses a rounded wooden lead; Snack Garden uses a related soft thumb-piano approximation. Both have a nylon-like triad accompaniment, a quiet root-based bass and one light brush pulse. These are synthesized approximations, not recordings of acoustic instruments. The maps have different original melodies and progressions.

## Arrangement and mix

Each 16-bar, 4/4 loop has four instrument parts and 131 note/percussion events. The previous tracks had eight parts and 689 / 557 events. A single three-note major/minor chord is strummed per bar; the former repeated syncopated chord strikes, countermelody, high bells, kick, rimshots and continuous shaker are removed. The lead now stays at G4–E5 in Sunny City and F4–D5 in Snack Garden, with written rests across 55.1% of the score. Brief passing notes connect ordinary chord tones.

The master uses linear gain, quiet short room reflections, and softened high frequencies. It adds no saturation, compressor pumping or long echo layer. Source RMS is targeted at −24.5 dBFS; decoded tracks are about 6 dB quieter than the earlier files, with over 10 dB of peak headroom. The rewrite changes the musical arrangement and timbres as well as loudness.

The existing **Music bus remains −14 dB**, and SFX routing, gains, cooldowns and voice counts remain unchanged. The new music's default post-bus RMS is about −38.5 dBFS, versus approximately −31 to −32 dBFS before. These are electrical signal measurements, not acoustic sound-pressure levels or proof of a pleasing perceived mix. Collection pings and positional food impacts still require actual-device listening to judge their balance.

## Runtime compatibility

The filenames are unchanged. Both resources remain stereo 44.1 kHz Ogg Vorbis, with a combined size of 294,922 bytes (about 48% smaller than before). The controller still uses one `AudioStreamPlayer` with `PLAYBACK_TYPE_STREAM`, duplicates the current map stream, enables `loop`, and sets `loop_offset=0`.

This rewrite does not alter the controller, bus layout, save schema or stored preferences. First gesture unlock, independent music/SFX mute, release-before-unlock ordering, pause and separate focus/suspension flags remain the existing implementation. Changing musical assets does not certify real browser audio unlock or speaker output.

## Rebuild and provenance

Requires Python 3 with NumPy and FFmpeg/FFprobe with libvorbis. Run locally:

```sh
python3 tools/build_music.py
```

Use `--output /absolute/directory` for a verification copy. The manifest records generator/toolchain hashes, exact PCM and Ogg hashes, every musical event and decoded audio measurements. The encoded Ogg files and entire manifest were regenerated byte-identically with the recorded local toolchain; see [rebuild_verification.json](rebuild_verification.json). Cross-version encoder byte identity is not claimed.

Circular note tails, quiet short reflections and periodic filtering preserve continuity, with a 2.5 ms cosine edge guard to limit Vorbis edge error. The final phrase resolves into the first. `LOOPSTART=0` and exact `LOOPEND` samples are embedded, and runtime explicitly configures looping. The 12-second MP3 previews are separate excerpts with a preview-only fade; they do not replace the full game loops.
