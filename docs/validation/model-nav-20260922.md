# Model-nav test scene · generated SAO-style models in a village square · 2026-09-22

Ten new textured models were generated through the user's own provider accounts, arranged as a plausible village market square alongside existing repository art, and used as real obstacles for engine-navigation NPCs. The verdict and all engine facts are in [acceptance.json](model-nav-20260922/acceptance.json); stills and the report video sit in this directory. No world save was touched, no Kimi call was made, and every model was generated exactly once with no appearance reroll.

## Scene layout

`game/scenes/model_nav_village.tscn` builds a settlement square from scratch: a 64 × 46 m grass ground (enlarged so no world edge enters frame), a 17 × 13 m raised stone plaza with an east-west market street in front of it, and the two generated buildings facing the square from the north. Props are placed where they would realistically belong rather than on a grid:

- the **stone well** stands at the plaza centre with the **bench** beside it and the **water trough** next to the well
- the **notice board** marks the plaza edge by the street; the **market stall**, **barrel**, **produce crate** and **handcart** form the market frontage along the street
- the **flower planter** sits under the market house window, the **hanging sign** hangs at the smithy frontage, the **flour sacks** are stacked by the shop's side door
- the **wagon wheel** leans in the smith's yard east of the building; three **street lanterns** (one generated model, two repeats) line the street
- surrounding detail reuses **21 existing repository assets** (fences, trees, berry bushes, wildflowers, mossy boulders, baking oven, market pieces), 23 instances loaded including the two lantern repeats

Each model is scaled so its largest bounding-box dimension matches its target size, y-aligned to the ground, and given per-mesh concave trimesh collision on a `StaticBody3D`. ## The house: the project's own authored building

The building is not modelled here. It is the repository's authored modular house **`03_corner_turret`** (`assets/floor1/modular_houses_20260916/03_corner_turret.glb`, three storeys, 9 × 7 m main block at 3.0 m floor height), instantiated through the project's own `spatial/modular_house_component.gd`. Everything architectural therefore comes from the asset: the real shell with **true wall openings**, its authored door and window pivots, its detached door leaf, and its real `BuildingShell` / `TimberFrame` trimesh collision. The scene reads the doorway from that component (`door_opening_godot()`) and swings the door open with `set_door_open(true)`, so NpcA walks through a visibly open door of the real asset; the roof collider and the door leaf are simply left out of the navigation source groups.

Only what the authored asset does not contain was added, and it is listed as such in the evidence:

- **one upper-floor slab** split around a stair opening, and
- **twelve staircase treads** rising 3.0 m from the ground floor to that slab.

Everything else inside the house is an existing `living_props_20260916` prop: table, two chairs, workbench, shelf, hearth and jug on the ground floor; bed, chest and a second jug on the second floor, plus three interior lamps. NpcB waits on the **second floor** at 3.0 m.

## NPC activity

| Actor | Behaviour | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NpcA | walks the street to the turret house, in through its open doorway, across the ground floor, up the staircase and onto the second floor, then turns to face NpcB | street (5.5, 0, −7.0) → NpcB upstairs |
| NpcB | waits on the second floor and turns to face NpcA when it arrives | fixed at (−5.5, 3.0, −13.6) |
| NpcC | carries water between the well and the shop door | plaza, 2.6 s dwell |
| NpcD | patrols the square perimeter past both buildings and the smith's yard | 4-point loop, 1.2 s dwell |
| NpcE | lingers by the notice board, then walks to the shop front | 2-point loop, 3.4 s dwell |

Every actor is a capsule-and-head marker with a named billboard tag, a step bob while walking and a slow idle bob while dwelling; the bob moves the body parts, never the root, so an actor's Y stays exactly on its route. No resident identity, save state or personality system is involved.

**How the vertical leg is routed.** The outdoor leg is engine pathfinding: a `NavigationAgent3D` walks the baked mesh from the street to the doorway. The authored shell carries **no walkable slab or stair geometry**, and the added treads are 0.3 m deep against roughly 0.45 m of agent-radius erosion, so the navigation bake cannot link floor to upper floor through the staircase. This scene therefore declares the climb the same way the repository's own `navigation_mvp_house` fixture does for stairs and ladders: an explicit nine-point connector chain carries NpcA through the doorway, across the room, up the stairs and onto the slab. The evidence for this leg is the actor's own measured height, not path geometry: `route_profile.max_y` = 2.99 m, which is the second-floor surface.

## Report video

[model-nav-report.mp4](model-nav-20260922/model-nav-report.mp4) is the captioned report: a title card, the pipeline flowchart, **12 seconds** of scene footage (orbit over the square, the market street with the house's open doorway, inside the house with the staircase, **the second-floor arrival where NpcA reaches NpcB**, closing exterior), and a summary card of the verified numbers — 33 seconds in total. In movie mode NpcA holds its walk so the interior shot records the real climb, and the planned route is drawn on the ground. Frame capture is used instead of the Godot movie writer (which froze part-way on this scene): the scene renders with its window **positioned off-screen** so reporting never takes over the user's desktop, saving viewport frames that ffmpeg encodes (H.264 / yuv420p). Cards come from `tools/build_model_nav_report_cards.py`, assembly from `tools/build_model_nav_report_video.py`; card sources sit beside the video in [cards/](model-nav-20260922/cards/).

## How to run

```powershell
.\Run-ModelNavVillage.ps1                               # interactive preview (third-person camera, route markers)
python tools\run_godot.py --godot <godot.exe> --name village -- --headless --script res://tests/model_nav_village_acceptance.gd
python tools\run_godot.py --godot <godot.exe> --name village-shots -- --position -2400,-1400 'res://scenes/model_nav_village.tscn' '--' '--shots'
# report footage: viewport frames saved with the window off-screen, then encoded by tools/build_model_nav_report_video.py
python tools\run_godot.py --godot <godot.exe> --name village-capture -- --position -2400,-1400 --resolution 1280x720 'res://scenes/model_nav_village.tscn' '--' '--movie' '--capture'
python -X utf8 tools\build_model_nav_report_cards.py
python -X utf8 tools\build_model_nav_report_video.py
```

Rendering always runs with the window positioned off-screen so reporting never takes over the desktop, and the Godot movie writer is deliberately not used: it froze part-way through this scene, which is why footage comes from viewport frames instead.

## Boundaries

- These are one-shot appearance candidates, not final commercial art, and no model was regenerated for looks. The single Meshy house is a style-contrast sample; the repository's H92 finding (Tripo houses read rounder/cartoon, Meshy props cleaner) is not re-tested here.
- Generated models arrive with their own front orientation; the layout keeps them at zero yaw so the narrow market-house facade faces the street. No per-model facing correction was applied.
- Collision is trimesh-per-mesh; the scene adds no interiors, doors or gameplay. The four actors are scene markers only: the ten residents, the canonical save and the Kimi loop are untouched.
- API model commercial terms and the MIT boundary are as recorded in `experiments/interior-first-pass/NOTICE.txt`; this batch changes nothing there.
